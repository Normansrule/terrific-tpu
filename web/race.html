The great matrix-multiply race.

Three machines with the same 16 multipliers compute the same matrix product. A plain processor does one multiply-accumulate (MAC) per clock; a vector unit does 16 in lockstep; the systolic array keeps 16 multipliers fed by passing numbers between neighbors. There are two prizes: fastest and least energy. They don't always go to the same machine, and that's the whole story of why TPUs exist.

Why speed and energy tell different stories

All three do exactly the same n³ multiply-accumulates with the same 16 multipliers. The idealized vector unit is often fastest here, because this model assumes its registers never run dry and TinyTPU's small 4 × 4 array pays 2N − 1 fill-and-drain cycles plus a weight load on every tile. But keeping those registers fed is exactly what costs energy:
