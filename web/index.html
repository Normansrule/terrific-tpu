A chip that only multiplies.

A Tensor Processing Unit (TPU) gives up nearly everything a normal processor can do, and spends its silicon on a grid of multipliers that pass numbers to their neighbors in rhythm. Watch the rhythm, then build one.

Each flash is one wave of data crossing the grid diagonally, one step per clock tick.

Systolic array

Edit a weight matrix and some input vectors, then step the clock and watch partial sums flow down the grid.

Whole chip

Write TinyTPU assembly and run it on a register-exact model of the Verilog: controller, skew, array, accumulators and memories.

Applications

Paint an image and watch 4 filters run; search with a 2-qubit quantum algorithm; count triangles in a graph you draw. All on the chip model.

3-D array

Spin an 8 × 8 (or 24 × 24) array in 3-D. Tower heights are live partial sums; the wavefront sweeps across as the clock ticks.

Numbers & roofline

See what 8-bit rounding does to a layer's weights, and whether a job keeps the multipliers busy or waits on memory.
